# Roadmap

Based on the research in the project (`research/character-base-options.md`, `research/makehuman-quality-config.md`).

## Done
- Actor pack format, importer, renderer-free runtime, three.js adapter, viewer, glTF export.
- All MakeHuman macro + detail sliders, adult age clamp in the data path.
- **Skin**: MakeHuman's 18 CC0 photo skins blended on the GPU by gender, age and ethnicity; own skin shader (pores, wrap-lighting subsurface approximation); procedural mode with region tints from MPFB masks.
- **Eyes, brows, lashes, teeth**: MakeHuman CC0 eyes (iris colour presets), MakeHuman CC0 eyebrow proxies (5 styles, painted brows as an option), procedural lashes, wet teeth/tongue materials.
- **Hair**: 64 MakeHuman hairstyles (9 system styles plus 24 CC0 and 31 CC-BY community styles, each in its own file and loaded when shown) as fitted proxies, alpha-to-coverage cards, recolourable.
- **Anatomy**: male geometry from the subdivided MakeHuman genital helper; female detail drawn procedurally in the skin shader. Adult-only, hidden under covering garments.
- **Detail**: Catmull-Clark subdivision at load time; dual-quaternion skinning.
- **Clothing**: generated garment library cut from the tights helper with per-pixel hems, plus a wardrobe of about 130 MakeHuman garments (tops, trousers, skirts, dresses, suits, complete outfits, underwear, hosiery, shoes, hats, glasses, gloves, and fantasy pieces: helmets, wizard and witch hats, robes, gowns; 30 of them CC-BY, credited in NOTICE-ASSETS.md and the editor) fitted by `.mhclo` binding, each in its own file and loaded when first worn. Skirt skinning (averaged thigh weights) and GPU leg colliders for skirts and dresses; body layers decide what replaces what; every garment recolourable (palette + free colour). A medieval kit (tunics, hose, gambeson, mail, surcoat, plate, jerkin, shoulder mantle) with twelve presets; no long cloaks until there is cloth simulation: docs/medieval-wardrobe.md.
- **Animation**: Quaternius Universal Animation Library clips (CC0, Unreal-mannequin skeleton with fingers and a stored rest pose) retargeted to any fitted skeleton (limbs aligned to the source rest pose, torso and clavicles keep the body's own posture); nine expression presets from MakeHuman's face units, auto-blink on GPU morphs.
- **Animation library**: all 84 usable Quaternius clips with categories; the CMU database (2,548 motions) as an on-demand clip library (BVH rest pose from the offsets, zero-length joints skipped, 16-bit clips, ~100 KB each), searchable in the editor.
- **Fantasy races** (from Norgo): ten races and two-race mixes; target recipes, bone proportion pass, head scale (capped for adult proportions), pointed ear tips and overall size in the core shape pipeline. Race looks: race-calibrated heights, recoloured photo skin, eye colours and glow, slit/goat pupils, skin patterns (scales, bark, veins, ...) in the skin shader. Add-ons: horns, tusks, tails, beards, leaf hair and crest, generated per shape and skinned to existing bones. Tails get a six-bone chain with an idle sway baked into every clip (exported to glTF); beards and tusks follow face expressions; painted stubble. Gaps: other Norgo procedural hairstyles (mohawk, topknot, dreadlocks...) still map to the nearest pack hair mesh; tail sway is a fixed idle wave (no physics).
- **Robots**: any character can be a robot on the same skeleton (every clip works) and the same body/race sliders. Generated from the current body (no CC0 robot asset is rigged to this skeleton or follows sliders): rigid armour plates cut from the skin where one bone dominates (gaps open at the joints), smoothed and thickened with rounded rims; the body surface as a flexible inner frame; hinge and ball housings at elbows, knees, hips, ankles and every finger joint; toe caps; abdominal actuator rings; a helmet fitted to the skull with visor, twin optics, mono-eye or android face, ear pods, antennae or fins; chest light. Plating styles (sleek, segmented, armoured), eight colour schemes, finishes and light colour/strength. Built in the shape worker (~0.7 s per shape change). Gaps: plates don't collide with each other in extreme poses; no facial animation for robots.
- **Skeletons**: undead skeletons on the human rig (every clip and garment works) that follow height, gender, proportions and race (horns and tusks stay). Bones are generated (no CC0 skeleton is rigged to this skeleton or follows sliders): skull with orbits, nasal aperture, cheekbones, sutures, teeth and a separate jaw; vertebrae, rib cage with cartilage and sternum, pelvis with sacrum, scapulae, clavicles, long bones with joint ends, full hands and feet. Options: robustness, wear, lost bones, lower jaw, eye and heart lights; seven colour palettes (ivory, bleached, crypt, bog, charred, lich, gilded). Built in the shape worker (~2 s per shape change). Gaps: no jaw animation (the rig has no jaw bone); bones are rigid, so extreme poses can overlap at the joints; the pack's high triangle count (~0.5 M) needs an LOD for crowds.
- **Generator**: `@actors/generator` packages everything as a reusable `Character` (JSON config, every clip, outfit presets from nude up, randomize, save/load, glTF export) with an optional stage and editor panel; the viewer is built on it.
- **Levels of detail**: full, medium (base-mesh body, garments/hair/eyes at about half their triangles via meshoptimizer) and crowd (MakeHuman's CC0 741-vertex proxy body, garments merged into one flat-coloured mesh, hair at a quarter, no finger bones), switched instantly or by camera distance; characters can share one loaded pack (`shareWith`). Gaps: no instancing or baked animation textures (every actor has its own shape and mixer); WebGL photo skins cost a 2k texture per actor, so large crowds should use the procedural skin; add-ons (horns, tails, beards) are not reduced or merged yet.
- **Animals (prototype)**: a dog generated on Mesh2Motion's CC0 quadruped rig with its 13 clips (idle, walk, run, sit, bark, howl, …), a Czechoslovakian Wolfdog base (the breed the body is modelled on), 12 more breed presets plus wolf and fox, bone-scale proportions, breed morphs and shell fur with procedural coat markings (`animals.html` in the viewer). A squirrel on the same rig and bones (eastern grey base, red, fox and black squirrel presets, its own clips: idle, alert, eat, hop, run, tail flick, plus all dog clips) at `animals.html?animal=squirrel`. A cat on the same rig and clips (`animals.html?animal=cat`): a Maine Coon modelled on one side-on photo (44k triangles, almond eyes with slit pupils, whiskers, ear tufts, toes, bushy tail), tabby stripes and tortoiseshell patches in the fur shader, and five coat presets. A deer (sika hind, modelled on a photo) on the rig and 13 clips of Quaternius' CC0 low-poly Deer (idle, eating, walk, gallop, leap, headbutt, kick, death, …), with sika spots in rows, dorsal stripe, rump patch and five more presets (fallow, chital, red deer, roe, white-tailed; `animals.html?animal=deer`). A ridable horse (bay gelding modelled on a photo, 64k triangles) on the rig and 13 clips of Mesh2Motion's CC0 horse (idle, walk, trot, gallop, turns, rear, kick, …), nine coat and type presets (bay, chestnut, black, dapple grey, palomino, buckskin, Arabian, draught horse, pony), optional saddle, bridle and stirrups, a `saddle` bone, and a `Mount` helper that seats a human in the `RIDE` pose (`animals.html?animal=horse`). A cow (Braunvieh modelled on a photo, 64k triangles) on the same horse rig and clips: udder, dewlap, cloven hooves, horns sized by morphs (polled to Highland), an optional alpine collar and bell, piebald patches, white faces and the mealy muzzle in the fur shader, and eight breed presets (Braunvieh, Holstein, Simmental, Hereford, Angus, Jersey, Highland, Charolais; `animals.html?animal=cow`). A chicken (brown laying hen modelled on a photo, 48k triangles) on the skeleton of Mesh2Motion's CC0 bird rig with its own clips (idle, walk, run, peck, scratch, flap, sit), drawn feathers in the fur shader (lacing, barring, hackle stripes, mottling), comb and wattle morphs and eight breed presets (brown hen, White Leghorn, Australorp, Barred Rock, Light Sussex, Buff Orpington, Silver Laced Wyandotte, Speckled Sussex; `animals.html?animal=chicken`).
- **Drones**: futuristic drones generated from building blocks (`@actors/drone`, `drones.html`, see docs/drones.md): six hull types, ducted fans, rotors, tilting jets, anti-grav rings and ion thrusters, sensors, tools, opt-in weapons, antennae, fins, gear, lights and twelve palettes, picked per seed and role into an editable JSON spec; looping clips (hover, cruise, scan, land, work, attack) export to glTF. Gaps: WebGL materials only; no collision checks between optional blocks, so rare combinations can overlap.
- **Performance**: shape evaluation in a Web Worker; subdivision computed at load time; targets streamed on demand; the importer pulls single files out of MakeHuman's asset-pack zips with HTTP range requests.

## Known gaps and next steps
1. **Anatomy quality**: the male helper mesh is low-poly even after subdivision. The only CC0 community replacement ("man genital" by ukiyoe) was tested and is not better: it has no scrotum and a pubic plate that shows as a raised patch on the subdivided body. Other community genitals are AGPL. Next step: a sculpted or commissioned CC0 mesh. Female detail is shader-only (no geometry).
2. **Corrective shapes**: DQS fixes volume loss; per-joint corrective morphs (shoulder raise, deep knee bend) would further improve extreme poses.
3. **Garments**: no cloth simulation. Combined outfits (e.g. shirt + jeans in one MakeHuman mesh) take one colour for the whole mesh; per-part colours would need part masks. Closed helmets and hoods hide the hair; open hats don't flatten it yet. Underwear fully covered by outer layers is not drawn; a garment can still show through another one on the same layer (a trouser waistband at a fitted top's hem). Long skirts stay outside the legs (colliders) but stretch in a wide stride; multi-layer skirts (tiers) are not supported by the colliders yet.
4. **Animation**: the free Standard editions have ~90 clips between them; only 13 are wired up. No facial animation in the clips (expressions are separate). The first retargeter used CMU BVH files without a real rest pose and broke shoulders and hands; clips now always come from sources with a stored rest pose, and `?compare` in the viewer shows the source next to the actor.
5. **Hair quality**: MakeHuman's hair cards are game-grade; strand-level or higher-density hair would need new assets.
6. **Download size**: the 9 system hair meshes still sit in the base blob (3.5 MB); the pack hairstyles are already loaded on demand.
7. **WebGPU**: done for three's WebGPURenderer (`@actors/three/webgpu`, node materials). Gaps: its shadow pass skins with linear blending (three draws shadows with its own depth material), and skin patterns read the bind pose before face morphs.
8. **Animals**: the dog body is a smooth sculpt from primitives; it still lacks anatomical detail (toes, dewclaws, muscle definition, lips and jowls), the shells show layering at grazing angles and in tight folds, and long coats (spaniel feathering, poodle curls) would need strands or cards. The cat has one body (no breed morphs yet besides bulk; bone proportions work), the fox's clips (no cat-specific motion such as grooming or a tail held up), and a mouth that only opens with the jaw. The deer has no jaw bone (closed mouth) and no antlers yet (a stag would need antler meshes on the head bone; Quaternius' Stag has the same rig). Cats and other quadrupeds can reuse the importer with their own primitive layout (Mesh2Motion's rig and clips also fit cats and foxes); birds and others need other rigs.
