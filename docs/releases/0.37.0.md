## Crowd figures: hundreds of people from a few baked bodies

Made for Nauta's two brig crews (about 240 men on an iPad under WebGPU), where a full character per man held about 7 MB of geometry, 1.6 GB for both crews.

- **`CrowdFigures`** (`@actors/three`) with **`createNodeCrowdFigureMaterial`** (`@actors/three/webgpu`): a few *kinds* (a build in an outfit, garment colours baked in) drawn as instances, one draw and one shadow draw per kind. Clips are baked once per build into a pose atlas texture and played on the GPU (interpolated between frames, cross-faded between clips); the CPU writes ten texels a man a frame. Each man has his own place, height, skin, hair and three garment-group colours, the hats and hairstyles he wears, his clip and phase, and an opacity for hand-overs (dithered, no sorting). Poses from outside (procedural motion, a full character's bones) go through a few live atlas rows (`pose`).
- **`buildCrowdFigures`** (`@actors/generator`) bakes builds and kinds from any character (`CharacterPatch` looks, optional hats and hairstyles, tint groups, clip ids), and **`crowdToCharacter` / `characterToCrowd`** hand a man over to a full character and back in the same pose: both use the same skeleton and clips (tested vertex by vertex).
- **Characters kept to some levels**: `Character.load({ ..., levels: [0, 1] })` (and `ThreeActor`'s `levels` option) makes no meshes of other levels: a pool of near characters holds no crowd mesh, a far one no subdivided body, lashes or teeth.
- `ThreeActor.crowdSkirts`: the crowd level merges skirt-skinned garments (coat tails) too, so every outfit is one draw (legs may show through in a stride).
- New viewer page `crowd.html` (two brig crews in the navy wardrobe, a pool of full characters) and check `apps/viewer/scripts/crowd-figures.mjs` (memory, frame cost, draw calls under WebGPU).

Node materials only (three's `WebGPURenderer`, WebGPU or its WebGL 2 backend). Existing packs work unchanged. See "Crowd figures" in docs/using-actors.md and docs/architecture.md.
