## Giants: proportions baked into the skin, every foot found, a stride paced to the ground

- **Deform without sheared limbs.** `LibraryAnimal.setDeform` used to put each part's length and girth on the bone scales. Bones inherit their parent's scale, so every limb that turned out of line with its parent sheared: the giant elk's hooves were drawn out into long spikes, its hind legs stood a hoof's height above the front ones, and the giant turtle's feet were flattened into sheets. The proportions are now baked once into a float copy of each mesh's skin (normals and tangents with it), the joints move out by their parent's length (also each frame, over the clips' position keys), and the bones keep a scale of 1. Meshes with their own skeleton and quantized meshes are handled; `setDeform(null)` and `dispose` give the original geometry back.
- **Every foot.** `FootGrounding` takes the soles of each leg from that leg's own lowest point (the lowest skin of a bone near a leaf, with nothing lower below it in the skeleton), not only the skin within 6% of the body's lowest point, which missed the elk's hind hooves. Skin that only keeps the body out of the ground (a belly, a plastron) no longer leans the body when there are feet. Chins are not feet.
- **A stride paced to the ground.** `strideSpeed(clip)` gives the ground speed at which this body plays a clip at its own gait (the clip's travel grown with the body and its legs, at `plan.gait.rate`); `pace(speed)` plays the current clip as fast as the ground goes by, within half and double that gait. A giant moved at about its stride speed neither skates nor leaves a foot hanging for seconds.
- **Empty clips are dropped.** A clip with no tracks once its root node's tracks are ignored (the library elk's `Walk` keyed only `RigRoot`) is no motion: playing it froze the animal in its bind pose. It is no longer in `clipNames`.

Measured on the library's elk (grown 6 times) and turtle (12.7 times) on flat ground and a 0.2 slope: standing, the elk's four soles are 0 to 0.24 m off the ground and the turtle's 0.1 to 0.26 m; walking at its stride speed every elk hoof touches the ground each stride.
