## A living face: eyes that look, lids that follow, lips that speak

The face rig (0.23.0) put every face unit on the GPU, but nothing moved it besides the expression presets and a fixed blink. A new, optional living face (`faceAnimation` in the character config, "Living face" in the editor) animates it:

- **Gaze.** The eyes get their own bones (`eye_l`, `eye_r` under the head, placed at the eyeball centres of each shape) and turn toward the camera (`gaze: "camera"`), straight ahead, or anything you pass to `character.lookAt` (a point or an object, followed every frame). Each eye aims on its own, so near points make them converge; turns are limited to what real eyes do. Because they are bones, it works on both renderers and in exported glTF skeletons.
- **Saccades and glances.** Small eye jumps every half second to two seconds while fixating, and now and then a glance away and back, often with a blink.
- **Blinks** with varied timing and speed (fast close, slower open), double blinks and half blinks.
- **Lids follow the eyes**: looking down lowers the upper lids, looking up lifts them.
- **Idle movement**: slow, small drift of brows, mouth corners, lids and nostrils.
- **Lip-sync**: `character.say(text)` (rough English letter rules), `character.speak(track)` for viseme tracks from a TTS engine (15 visemes, `VISEMES`), and `character.setVoiceLevel(level)` to move the jaw with audio loudness. The editor's "Say" box tries it.

Eyes turn with or without the face rig; lids, idle movement and lip-sync need `faceRig: true`. The animator is renderer-free (`FaceAnimator` in `@actors/human`) and costs about 0.02 ms per character and frame on the CPU. Everything is off by default; no pack change is needed.
