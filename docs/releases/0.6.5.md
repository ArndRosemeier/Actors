## What's new: one shader per material kind on three's WebGPURenderer

With `nodeMaterials` (`@actors/three/webgpu`), every character used to build its own shaders, because three's node renderer keys a shader by the identity of a material's nodes. Ten characters meant ten skin shaders, ten eye shaders, and so on. Now each material kind builds one graph that all characters share.

- **Shared graphs.** Skin, eyes, garment tint and cut-outs, hair tint, hem clipping, leg colliders and the crowd mesh build their nodes once. A material's own values (tones, tints, photo-skin layers, masks) are read at each draw from `material.userData.actorInputs`.
- **Face morphs as plain attributes.** The `expression` and `blink` channels are vertex attributes (`actorMorph<k>`, `actorMorph<k>N`) blended in the skinning step and weighted by `mesh.morphTargetInfluences`. Three's morph targets used to make each face mesh's shader its own. The kit flag `attributeMorphs` is on in `nodeMaterials`.
- Measured in Dunfinity (WebGL 2 backend, headless): a lineup of every race went from 262 shaders built in warm-up to 83, and a lineup of ghosts from 250 to 69. Looks are unchanged side by side.
- `webglMaterials` (WebGLRenderer) is unchanged: it already shared its programs.

## How to use it

Nothing to change: `materials: nodeMaterials` shares shaders on its own. Code that recolours a garment or hair keeps working through `userData.tint` / `userData.hair` (`{ value }` holders now, instead of TSL uniforms).
