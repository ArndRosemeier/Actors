## Skin that reads less like plastic

Skin was one even colour with one even shine, so faces looked like smooth plastic or wax up close. A new, optional `skinDetail` setting ("Skin detail" under Look, on by default) adds the variation real skin has, for photo and procedural skins alike:

- **Blood flush** on the cheeks, nose, ears and chin, and around the knees, elbows and hands.
- **A soft shadow under the eyes.**
- **Shine where skin is oily**: the forehead and nose are glossier, the rest a little rougher, and roughness varies at a fine scale, so highlights break up instead of sliding over the body like a plastic sheen.
- **Pores** show more on the nose and cheeks, over a second, finer layer of skin texture.
- **Fine pigment speckle.**

The face regions are placed from the eyes (the same face frame as painted stubble) and the limb regions from the skeleton's knees, elbows and wrists, so they follow every body shape and race. Photo skins already carry some colour variation, so the flush is lighter on them. Both renderers have it (WebGL and WebGPU). It costs a few dozen shader instructions and one texture read per skin pixel. `ThreeActor.skin.uDetail` (0..1) drives it directly, and `apps/viewer/scripts/skin.mjs` shoots close-ups with it on and off.
