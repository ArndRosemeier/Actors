## Fix: people and animals draw again on real WebGPU

0.6.5 broke every person and animal on three's WebGPURenderer with real WebGPU (its WebGL 2 backend was fine). The face morphs it moved into vertex attributes took four vertex buffers of their own, which pushed a face mesh to 9 vertex buffers; WebGPU allows 8, so those draws failed ("Vertex buffer count (9) exceeds the maximum number of vertex buffers (8)").

- **Face morphs share one vertex buffer.** The `expression` and `blink` channels (positions and normals) are now interleaved into a single buffer per mesh, so they cost one slot instead of four. Measured on real WebGPU (SwiftShader): every race, dressed and smiling, now peaks at 7 vertex buffers (was 9); dogs, cats and squirrels at 5 (was 8, right at the limit). The attribute names and the shared shaders from 0.6.5 are unchanged.
- **Races with a tail on WebGPU.** Switching to a race whose tail adds bones (drakeborn and others) made WebGPU reject the larger bone upload ("Write range ... does not fit"), because three kept the old, smaller bone buffer. The affected meshes now rebuild their draws when the bone count changes. This one was older than 0.6.5.

## How to use it

Nothing to change. If you switched off `attributeMorphs` to work around 0.6.5, turn it back on (or drop the override): it is what lets every character share one shader per material kind.

To check a page on real WebGPU headless: `node apps/viewer/scripts/webgpu-check.mjs "http://localhost:5173/?webgpu" out.png --human` (or `webgpu-animals.html` for animals). It fails on any WebGPU error or any pipeline over the vertex buffer limit.
