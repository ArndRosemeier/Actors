## Library animals: a standing foot meets the ground with its lowest point too; no foot left in a cliff's edge

- **Standing feet.** 0.33.2 brought a walking foot down by its lowest point. A standing foot was still laid as it lies at rest, each sole point its rest height above the ground. A clip that holds the foot tilted (the library turtle's idle) stood it on its rest sole, so the turtle hung 0.1 to 0.3 m over the ground. Standing feet now move as one too, by as much as brings their lowest point to the ground. On flat ground and on a 0.2 slope the turtle's idle feet are at 0 to 0.02 m, and the elk's idle hooves are no higher than before. Only skin that keeps the body out of the ground (a plastron, a belly) is still laid by its rest heights.
- **Broken ground.** A walking foot gets a fourth reach round. The leg turns a foot while bringing it down, and at a cliff's edge under a giant's foot three rounds left it 2% of the body's size in the rock. That was MasterWorld's ground check on a cliff coast; it is now 1.3%, and other places float less too. A round is only run when a foot still needs it: the elk's and the turtle's walks cost the same as before.
