## Library animals: a walking foot meets the ground with its lowest point

The giant turtle's feet hovered 0.3 to 0.7 m above the sand while it walked (its left hand never touched it). Two things held them up:
- The skin of a leg above its sole (a calf swung back near the ground) counted as body skin that must stay out of the ground, so it lifted the whole body. A leg's own skin is now left to the leg.
- A walking foot was laid on the ground as it lies at rest (each sole point its rest height above it), but a walk turns the foot (a heel lifted, the toes reaching), so the point meant to touch was not the lowest. Walking, the foot now moves as one, by as much as brings its lowest point to the ground; standing, the sole is still laid flat. The planted feet reach down in every round, not only the first.

On the library turtle (grown 12.7 times) walking on flat ground, each foot now comes down to 0 to 0.13 m (it was 0.28 to 0.41 m at best); the elk is unchanged.
